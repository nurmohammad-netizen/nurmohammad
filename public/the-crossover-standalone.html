It's a gap of just 254 TWh — less than half a percentage point — but the direction is what matters: coal generation fell for only the fifth time this century, while solar and wind alone met 99% of the world's new electricity demand. Renewables supplied 18.7% of global electricity in 2000. In 2025, for the first time, they supplied more than coal.

      Battery costs fell 45% in 2025, on top of a 20% drop in 2024. Deployment grew to roughly 250 GWh — enough to shift 14% of new solar generation into hours after the sun goes down.

      On the ground

      • CHINA

        Fossil generation fell 56 TWh (−0.9%) — its first annual decline since 2015 — as solar and wind reached 22% of the country's generation mix.

      • INDIA

        Fossil generation fell 52 TWh (−3.3%), its first decline this century. India also installed more new solar capacity than the United States for the first time.

      • WORLD

        Coal's share has been sliding since it peaked at 41% in 2013. 2025 was only the fifth year this century that fossil generation failed to grow.
